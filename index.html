Now

October 2026

I'm working through how LLM tooling fits into writing and maintaining tests at scale: where generated tests hold up, where they quietly rot, and what review has to look like when a model writes the first draft.

I treat this as an engineering leadership problem more than a tooling one. The interesting question isn't which assistant is best. It's what changes about code review, ownership and how engineers grow when the first draft is cheap. This year it became a talk at DevTalks: designing reliable AI-generated test automation using proven design patterns.

What I work on

Three areas, one goal: teams that can change software quickly without being afraid of it.

Long bio 129 words

Sargis Sargsyan is Senior Test Architecture Manager at Align Technology in Yerevan, Armenia, with more than 17 years in software quality. Before Align he was Director of Quality Engineering at Picsart, where he built and led the quality engineering organisation. Earlier he was a Principal Software Engineer in Test at Workfront (now Adobe Workfront) and a Senior Quality Engineer at Auctionata in Berlin. His talks focus on making large test suites fast and trustworthy: dockerized grids, atomic test design, test data strategy and in-house device labs, and most recently on keeping AI-generated tests reliable with proven design patterns. Since 2015 he has given more than 30 talks and workshops at conferences including SeleniumConf, Appium Conf, STARWEST, HUSTEF, DevTalks, Agile Testing Days and Nordic Testing Days, in English and Russian.
